What the audit can and cannot establish

  • Checks explicit source-file SHA-256 digests, key/value assertions bound to source text, required graph links, revision flags, readiness approval/time, test scheduling order, and contradictions between a readiness certificate and explicitly open issues.
  • Requires your responsible reviewer to identify the actual executed contract clauses, approved submittals, and owner checklist. SABLE does not automatically determine whether a generic or public requirement applies to your project.
  • Current v1 reads a JSON graph manifest and local UTF-8 text sidecars with explicit field mappings. It does not directly parse PDFs/CAD, run equipment tests, connect to BMS/EPMS/SCADA/PLC, authenticate human signatures cryptographically, interpret engineering measurements, authorize energization, or certify safety.
  • A VERIFIED verdict means only that the declared v1 evidence-structure checks passed. It is not a commissioning certificate, engineering opinion, acceptance approval, or guarantee that a system is safe or operationally ready.

Why evidence state matters

A 2021 Chicago Office of Inspector General audit reviewed five public-building projects. It found one project missing most commissioning documentation and also reported cases where staff still lacked complete documents or could not access a shared platform. This is a historical public audit—not a SABLE customer result or proof of current demand—and it illustrates why document existence, delivery, and recipient access are different states.
