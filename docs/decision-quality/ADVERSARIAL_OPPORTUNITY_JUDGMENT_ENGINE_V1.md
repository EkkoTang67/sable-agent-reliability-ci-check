# Adversarial Opportunity & Judgment Engine (AOJE) v1.0

Status: ACTIVE — manual/AI-assisted operating protocol
Parent: Decision Error Correction System (DECS) v1.0
Default budget: 0
Purpose: use disciplined reverse thinking to discover valuable opportunities, attack weak assumptions, and improve strategic position without confusing intensity with progress.

## 1. Operating doctrine

**Attack assumptions, not people. Attack bottlenecks, not safety boundaries. Increase learning velocity, not suffering or uncontrolled workload.**

AOJE has two equally important missions:

1. Defensive: find how our current belief, plan, product, or transaction could fail before it becomes expensive.
2. Offensive: find neglected constraints, asymmetric openings, broken incentives, emerging bottlenecks, and positions where a small intervention can unlock disproportionate value.

A high-activity day with no uncertainty reduced is not progress. A rejected hypothesis that saves a month is progress.

## 2. The reverse-thinking stack

For every material opportunity or strategic choice, run these lenses:

### A. Inversion — how would we guarantee failure?
- What five actions would make this fail?
- Which of those are we already doing?
- What hidden dependency could invalidate the whole plan?
- What is the smallest early signal of failure?

Convert each credible failure mode into a test, control, or stop condition.

### B. Anti-customer — why would nobody buy?
- Who has the pain but no budget?
- Who has budget but no urgency?
- Who can solve it internally or bypass us?
- Who bears the cost while someone else captures the benefit?
- What would make procurement reject us even if the work is useful?
- What exact event would cause a buyer to pay now rather than later?

Do not label a market as validated until a real payer, authority, budget, eligibility, acceptance condition, and payment path are supported.

### C. Competitor inversion — how would a stronger rival defeat us?
- What can an incumbent bundle for free?
- What data, distribution, trust, regulation, or switching cost do they control?
- What would they copy immediately?
- What would they refuse to build, and why?
- Which narrow workflow is too small, awkward, urgent, or cross-boundary for them to prioritize?
- Can we partner, supply a component, or own an evidence/decision interface instead of competing head-on?

### D. Constraint inversion — what becomes scarce when something gets cheaper?
When AI reduces the cost of cognition or production, inspect what becomes the new bottleneck:
- trusted inputs and provenance;
- integration with physical operations and legacy systems;
- authorization and accountability;
- quality, acceptance, and liability;
- verification of productivity gains;
- distribution, procurement, and customer access;
- energy, compute, equipment, and deployment capacity;
- settlement, reconciliation, and cross-company coordination.

Do not assume any listed bottleneck is a business. Find a current decision, economic consequence, payer, and non-bypassable need.

### E. Power and value-capture inversion
Map who:
- creates value;
- controls the customer relationship;
- owns critical data and workflow entry points;
- sets standards and acceptance criteria;
- controls budget and procurement;
- bears downside risk;
- captures margin and recurring revenue;
- can block access or change the rules.

Ask what small asset, interface, evidence layer, or capability could shift our position in this map.

### F. Opportunity inversion — what if the obvious product is wrong?
- What outcome is the buyer actually purchasing?
- What must be true immediately before money, access, goods, release, or acceptance moves?
- What unresolved UNKNOWN blocks that action?
- Could a tiny service, dataset, test, standard, or workflow prove value before software exists?
- What adjacent industry has the same underlying failure pattern but a clearer payer?

### G. Self-inversion — how might our own reasoning be distorted?
Check for:
- attraction to dramatic or fashionable narratives;
- confusing a large project budget with our addressable subcontract;
- mistaking public intent for buyer commitment;
- mistaking a reply, quote, PO, invoice, or payment request for settled revenue;
- defending an old strategy because of sunk cost;
- adding agents and architecture to avoid buyer contact;
- confusing more research with stronger evidence;
- overcorrecting into excessive caution and killing options too early.

## 3. Mandatory opportunity attack card

For every candidate that survives initial screening, record:

1. Candidate and exact decision to be changed.
2. Current economic event and deadline.
3. Buyer, decision owner, payer, and payment authority — each separately.
4. Evidence for the pain and evidence against it.
5. Current workaround and bypass options.
6. Why the workaround is insufficient now.
7. Budget evidence and whether it is actually addressable by our proposed work.
8. Outsourcing evidence and who can authorize a subcontract.
9. Supplier eligibility, data authorization, legal/tax constraints, and payment rail.
10. Strongest kill hypothesis.
11. Cheapest discriminating test.
12. Maximum cash, hours, access, and attempts.
13. Explicit continue / reduce / park / kill thresholds.
14. Reusable asset or strategic position if successful.
15. What new evidence would change the verdict.

Unknown is a valid answer. Unsupported completion is prohibited.

## 4. Three attack rounds

### Round 1 — Destroy the story
The Red Team assumes the opportunity is false. It must produce specific, testable reasons, not generic risk language.

### Round 2 — Rebuild from observed reality
The Discovery Team ignores the original product idea and starts from verified events, constraints, incentives, and buyer actions. It proposes alternative value-capture points.

### Round 3 — Design the smallest asymmetric move
The Strategy Team selects the smallest lawful, reversible action that can:
- reach a real decision owner or payer;
- reveal a critical unknown;
- produce evidence a buyer values;
- create an option to expand if successful;
- preserve the ability to stop cheaply.

The independent reviewer compares all three rounds and records dissent. Agreement between AI agents is not independent evidence if they share sources or assumptions.

## 5. Attack cadence and resource discipline

- D0: quick inversion for trivial decisions.
- D1: one short attack card before a bounded experiment.
- D2: full three-round review before external claims, proposals, or material time commitments.
- D3: independent verification and explicit human authorization before irreversible, legal, financial, safety, or sensitive-data actions.

Default sequence:
OBSERVE → INVERT → TEST → UPDATE → ACT → REVIEW.

Use time-boxes. Do not turn “go harder” into sleep deprivation, unlimited research, repeated outreach, or uncontrolled spending. The system should reduce the operator's cognitive and physical burden through AI synthesis and automation, not increase it.

## 6. Decision output contract

Every material AOJE review returns:

- **Opportunity thesis:** one falsifiable sentence.
- **Best offensive opening:** the narrowest plausible leverage point.
- **Strongest kill case:** the most damaging credible counter-hypothesis.
- **Power map:** who controls access, budget, evidence, acceptance, and margin.
- **Critical unknowns:** ranked by decision impact.
- **Cheapest discriminating test:** exact action and what each result means.
- **Exposure cap:** cash, time, access, attempts, deadline.
- **Verdict:** ATTACK / TEST / REDUCE_SCOPE / PARK / KILL / HUMAN_APPROVAL.
- **Update trigger:** evidence that changes the verdict.
- **Asset path:** what reusable capability or position could compound if validated.

## 7. Metrics

Track alongside DECS:
- median time from opportunity signal to first disconfirming test;
- percentage of candidates killed or materially reshaped before expensive work;
- percentage of surviving candidates with payer and authority independently confirmed;
- false-kill rate (promising opportunities rejected without adequate test);
- cost per decision-changing piece of evidence;
- repeated strategic blind spots;
- operator hours saved by AI synthesis and automation;
- proportion of effort that reaches external reality rather than internal architecture.

Do not set arbitrary numerical targets before establishing a baseline.

## 8. Hard boundaries

AOJE does not authorize deception, harassment, unauthorized access, privacy violations, market manipulation, evasion of law, unsafe experiments, or uncontrolled financial risk. Competitive analysis uses lawful, authorized, and appropriately public or licensed evidence.

**Governing principle: Be aggressive toward uncertainty; be conservative with irreversible exposure. Seek asymmetric upside through evidence, not bravado.**
